Section I

Species Notes

Ten freshwater residents worth knowing before you buy — what water they need, how big they get, who they can live with, and the common varieties sold under each name.

The specimens

10 entries
No. 01 · Anabantoidei

Betta

Betta splendens

Native to shallow rice paddies, prized for flowing fins and a short temper toward other males. A labyrinth organ lets it breathe surface air, which is why it survives in water many fish couldn't.

OriginThailand, Mekong basin
Adult size2.5–3 in
Temperature76–82°F
pH6.5–7.5
Min. tank5 gal, one male alone
TemperamentTerritorial, solitary
DietCarnivore — pellets, live/frozen
Easy
Common varieties
Veiltail —the long-trailing tail seen in most pet stores; easiest fins to keep intact.
Halfmoon —tail opens to a full 180°; heavy finnage needs pristine water to avoid rot.
Crowntail —spiky, rayed fins; keep decor smooth since the rays snag and tear easily.
Plakat —short-finned, closest to the wild type; hardiest and the strongest swimmer.
No. 02 · Characidae

Neon Tetra

Paracheirodon innesi

A blackwater schooling fish whose iridescent blue-red stripe is a signal to the rest of the shoal. Kept alone, it hides; kept in numbers, it's one of the most active fish in the tank.

OriginAmazon basin, Peru/Brazil
Adult size1.5 in
Temperature72–78°F
pH6.0–7.0
Min. tank10 gal, shoal of 6+
TemperamentPeaceful, schooling
DietOmnivore — micro pellets, flake
Easy
Common varieties
Cardinal Tetra —red stripe runs the full body length; prefers slightly warmer, softer water.
Green Neon Tetra —smaller and more muted in color; same schooling and water needs.
Diamond-head Neon —a sparkly forehead-scale mutation, otherwise identical care.
No. 03 · Callichthyidae

Corydoras Catfish

Corydoras spp.

A bottom-dwelling scavenger that patrols the substrate in tight groups, using barbels to feel out food. Bare or sharp gravel wears down those barbels — sand is kinder.

OriginSouth American river bottoms
Adult size2–2.5 in
Temperature72–78°F
pH6.5–7.5
Min. tank20 gal, group of 6+
TemperamentPeaceful, bottom-dweller
DietOmnivore — sinking wafers
Easy
Common varieties
Bronze Cory —the classic, hardiest species; tolerates the widest temperature swings.
Panda Cory —black eye-patch marking; prefers it cooler, 72–75°F, than most corys.
Sterbai Cory —spotted, warm-water lover — a good bottom-dweller for discus tanks.
No. 04 · Poeciliidae

Guppy

Poecilia reticulata

Livebearers that breed readily in almost any stable water — a single female can produce fry monthly. Popular as a starter fish precisely because they're so forgiving.

OriginNortheast South America
Adult size1.5–2.4 in
Temperature74–82°F
pH7.0–8.0
Min. tank10 gal
TemperamentPeaceful, prolific breeder
DietOmnivore
Easy
Common varieties
Fancy Guppy —large flowing tail bred for color shows; fins tear easily around nippers.
Endler's Guppy —a smaller, hardier close relative; less prone to explosive overbreeding.
Common/Feeder Guppy —plain-finned and inexpensive, but just as hardy — a solid starter fish.
No. 05 · Cichlidae

Angelfish

Pterophyllum scalare

A tall, laterally-flattened cichlid that pairs off and can turn surprisingly territorial around spawning time. Needs vertical space more than floor space.

OriginAmazon basin
Adult sizeUp to 6 in tall
Temperature78–84°F
pH6.5–7.5
Min. tank29 gal, tall
TemperamentSemi-aggressive
DietOmnivore — eats small fish
Moderate
Common varieties
Silver Angelfish —the classic wild-type stripe pattern; the most robust of the strains.
Veil Angelfish —extra-long trailing fins; more prone to nipping and fin damage.
Koi / Marble Angelfish —a mottled orange-black color morph with identical care needs.
No. 06 · Loricariidae

Otocinclus

Otocinclus spp.

A small algae-grazer that struggles in brand-new tanks with no biofilm to eat. Best added once a tank is a few months established, not on day one.

OriginSouth America
Adult size1.5–2 in
Temperature72–79°F
pH6.5–7.5
Min. tank10 gal, group of 5+
TemperamentPeaceful grazer
DietHerbivore — algae, biofilm
Moderate
Common varieties
Common Oto —the widely-sold species; still needs an established tank to acclimate.
Zebra Oto —bold striped pattern, rarer in the trade, equally delicate on arrival.
Dwarf Oto —smaller still; best suited to heavily planted, low-flow nano tanks.
No. 07 · Cobitidae

Kuhli Loach

Pangio kuhlii

An eel-bodied, nocturnal burrower that spends daylight hours wedged under decor. A tight-lidded tank matters — it can and will slip through surprisingly small gaps.

OriginSoutheast Asia
Adult size3–4 in
Temperature75–86°F
pH5.5–6.5
Min. tank20 gal, group of 5+
TemperamentPeaceful, nocturnal
DietOmnivore — sinking foods
Moderate
Common varieties
Striped Kuhli —the classic black-and-orange banding; the most widely available.
Black Kuhli —solid dark coloring, same burrowing habits and tank needs.
Panda Kuhli —rarer black-and-white pattern; more sensitive to water quality swings.
No. 08 · Atyidae

Cherry Shrimp

Neocaridina davidi

A hardy dwarf shrimp bred for deep red coloring, useful as a cleanup crew for biofilm and leftover food. Extremely sensitive to copper — check every medication label.

OriginTaiwan
Adult size1–1.5 in
Temperature65–78°F
pH6.5–8.0
Min. tank5 gal
TemperamentPeaceful, copper-sensitive
DietOmnivore — biofilm, wafers
Easy
Common grades
Sakura grade —solid but lighter red; the easiest grade to breed true over generations.
Fire Red grade —deep, opaque red used for shows; same care as any Neocaridina.
Wild-type —brown-green base color; the hardiest starting point for a colony.
No. 09 · Loricariidae

Bristlenose Pleco

Ancistrus spp.

A stout algae-eater that stays a fraction of the size of a common pleco, making it the tank-safe choice. Appreciates driftwood to rasp on — it aids digestion.

OriginSouth America
Adult size4–5 in
Temperature73–79°F
pH6.5–7.5
Min. tank20 gal
TemperamentPeaceful, own-species territorial
DietHerbivore — wafers, driftwood
Easy
Common varieties
Common (brown) —the standard, hardiest form and the easiest to find.
Albino —pale body with pink eyes; keep lighting a bit dimmer than usual.
Longfin —an ornamental fin trait bred on top of the standard form; same diet and tank size.
No. 10 · Cichlidae

Discus

Symphysodon spp.

A tall, disc-shaped cichlid that demands pristine, warm, soft water and is unforgiving of neglected maintenance. Usually kept by aquarists who already have a few tanks behind them.

OriginAmazon River
Adult size6–8 in
Temperature82–86°F
pH6.0–7.0
Min. tank55 gal, group of 6
TemperamentPeaceful, sensitive
DietCarnivore — high-protein
Advanced
Common varieties
Blue Discus —closest to the wild coloring, and slightly more forgiving than show strains.
Red Turquoise / Pigeon Blood —intense show colors; the most demanding of pristine water.
Checkerboard Discus —a patterned strain with the same high-maintenance needs as other domestics.
