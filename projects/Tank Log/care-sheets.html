The routines

Setup

Cycling a New Tank

  1. Fill, add dechlorinator, and run the filter for 24 hours before anything else.
  2. Add a source of ammonia — a fishless-cycle dose, or a few hardy fish — to feed bacteria growth.
  3. Test daily: ammonia and nitrite will rise, then fall as nitrite converts to nitrate.
  4. Consider it cycled once ammonia and nitrite both read zero within 24 hours of dosing.
  5. Do a large water change before adding a full stocking list — nitrate builds up fast in a new cycle.
Maintenance

Weekly Water Change

  1. Unplug the heater and any submerged equipment before starting.
  2. Vacuum the substrate while draining 10–20% of the water — this pulls out settled waste, not just water.
  3. Refill with dechlorinated water matched as closely as possible to the tank's temperature.
  4. Wipe the inside glass and rinse (don't scrub) mechanical filter media if it's clogging flow.
  5. Log the date — consistency matters more than any single change being large.
Daily

Feeding Schedule

  • Feed most community fish once or twice daily, only what's gone within 2 minutes.
  • Skip a feeding day once a week for adult fish — it mimics natural feast-and-famine and curbs bloat.
  • Target sinking food to bottom-dwellers separately so they aren't out-competed at the surface.
  • Watch fry and juveniles more closely — they need smaller, more frequent meals than adults.
Biosecurity

Quarantine Protocol

  1. Set up a bare-bottom quarantine tank with a sponge filter seeded from an established tank.
  2. House new arrivals there for 2–4 weeks, observing for spots, clamped fins, or unusual breathing.
  3. Treat proactively only if you see symptoms — not every fish needs blanket medication.
  4. Use separate nets and equipment between quarantine and display tanks to avoid cross-contamination.
  5. Only move fish to the display tank after a full symptom-free period.
Monitoring

Water Testing Checklist

  • Ammonia and nitrite: should read zero in an established tank — any reading means investigate immediately.
  • Nitrate: rising steadily between water changes is normal; a sudden spike points to overfeeding or a dead fish.
  • pH: test at the same time of day, since it naturally drifts with the tank's CO2 level over 24 hours.
  • GH/KH: worth checking once when setting up, especially for cichlids or shrimp with specific hardness needs.
  • Test weekly in an established tank, daily during a cycle or after any sign of trouble.
Troubleshooting

Algae, By Type

  • Green water (a cloudy green tint): usually excess light or nutrients — cut photoperiod and feeding first.
  • Brown diatoms on glass: common in new tanks, typically fades on its own within a few weeks.
  • Green spot or hair algae: often a phosphate or CO2 imbalance in a planted tank — check dosing before adding more light.
  • Black beard algae: thrives on inconsistent CO2 — stabilizing injection usually does more than any product.
