Tank Setups

Seven blueprints, from a shrimp bowl on a desk to a CO2-injected scape — pick the one that matches your space, budget, and patience.

The blueprints

7 entries
Nano

Shrimp Bowl

A small, densely planted vessel built around cherry or Amano shrimp rather than fish. Low bioload means it's forgiving of a slow start, but sudden parameter swings hit shrimp harder than fish.

5 gal size Sponge filter filtration Shrimp, snails livestock Easy difficulty
Classic

Freshwater Community

The default first "real" tank — a mixed group of peaceful schooling fish, a few bottom-dwellers, and enough plants to take the edge off water changes. Everything in the species notes above fits somewhere in this tank.

20–29 gal size HOB or canister filtration Tetras, corydoras, livebearers livestock Easy difficulty
Planted

Low-Tech Planted

Shade-tolerant plants like anubias, java fern, and crypts, grown under modest light with root tabs or liquid fertilizer instead of injected CO2. Slower growth, but far less to manage day to day.

10–20 gal size Low–medium lighting None CO2 Moderate difficulty
Planted

High-Tech, CO2-Injected

Pressurized CO2, strong lighting, and a fertilizer dosing routine (commonly "Estimative Index") aimed at fast, lush plant growth. Rewarding, but the parameters need real attention — CO2 mismanagement can suffocate fish overnight.

20–40 gal size High lighting Pressurized CO2 Advanced difficulty
Solo

Species-Only Betta

Built around a single betta's preference for calm water and its intolerance for fin-nipping tankmates. Gentle, baffled filtration and floating plants for cover make the biggest difference in day-to-day stress.

5–10 gal size Gentle sponge filtration One betta, optional snails livestock Easy difficulty
Cichlid

African Rift Lake

Mbuna and similar cichlids from Lake Malawi or Tanganyika need hard, alkaline water — the opposite of what most South American fish want — plus heavy rockwork to break sightlines and diffuse aggression.

55+ gal size Hard, high pH water Mbuna cichlids livestock Moderate–Advanced difficulty
Marine

Reef, Introductory

Live rock, a protein skimmer, and stable salinity form the backbone before a single fish goes in. Soft corals and clownfish are the usual entry point into saltwater — everything moves slower here, including mistakes forgiving themselves.

40+ gal size Skimmer + live rock filtration Clownfish, soft corals livestock Advanced difficulty
